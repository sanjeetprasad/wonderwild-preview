Authoring status: draft for educator review. A reference means the skill is addressed in a lesson or activity. It does not establish that every clause of the standard is sufficiently taught, practiced, or assessed. This plan supplements adult instruction and independent reading; it is not a certified school replacement.

What the daily plan contains

Each day combines math, literacy, an investigation or writing task, and independent reading. Lessons recur for modeling, practice, explanation, and review. The 720 scheduled activities are not 720 unique lessons. There are 112 added lesson records: 36 math units, four sampled checkpoints, 36 three-page mini-readers, and 36 investigations.

Adults choose suitable independent books, provide safe materials, discuss work, and adjust pacing. The app records participation and first-try checks; these are not validated measures of mastery. Four math checkpoints sample skills rather than testing every standard.

Literacy and language

Weeks 1–16 emphasize spelling patterns and rereading. Weeks 17–24 emphasize literary details, structure, rhythm, perspective, and comparison. Weeks 25–31 emphasize information texts and evidence. Weeks 32–36 emphasize writing, revision, and sharing. Language practice is spread across 18 two-week focuses.

Source strands: foundational reading, literature, information texts, writing, speaking and listening, and language.

Review needs: independently level the original texts; broaden culturally varied literature, folktales, and paired versions; verify systematic phonics sequence and decodability; provide specialist sound recordings; review the depth of writing, discussion, fluency, and comprehension assessment. Daily independent reading requires books beyond the built-in mini-readers.

Science and social studies

The 18 science investigations address materials, plant needs, seed movement and pollination models, habitat variety, Earth changes, erosion, land/water models, water locations, and engineering design. Intended connections include 2-PS1-1–4, 2-LS2-1–2, 2-LS4-1, 2-ESS1-1, 2-ESS2-1–3, and K-2-ETS1-1–3. See the official NGSS K–2 standards.

Hands-on evidence is necessary: a multiple-choice check alone does not demonstrate a science performance expectation. Adults should review test design, observations, explanations, safety, and model limitations.

The 18 social-studies investigations cover community roles, sources and timelines, rules and decisions, goods and services, choices, maps, directions, landforms, globes, and connections between places. This is a general sequence. A target jurisdiction and local history requirements must be chosen before state alignment can be claimed.

Speech capability

Read-aloud uses the browser’s synthetic voice. Optional speech recognition compares text recognized by the browser and may involve its online provider. The separate 15-second recorder keeps audio in page memory for replay and clears it when leaving the lesson. Neither feature measures phoneme accuracy.

Accurate pronunciation feedback still requires a dedicated assessment service, secure credentials, a reviewed child-data flow, and evaluation with children’s speech, dialects, and accents. No such service is connected in this edition.

Release review still required

Before presenting this as a complete validated curriculum: an educator should review every standard’s full expectation, lesson depth, daily workload, text accessibility, assessment reliability, and supplementary reading. Pilot the flow with families, collect feedback with consent, and revise pacing and teaching strategies. Other K–6 levels currently retain their starter collections.
